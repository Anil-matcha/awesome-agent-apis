# Research Brief

**Category:** Research and knowledge<br>
**Integrations:** Muse web browsing, notes, files, and optional reference library<br>
**Approval level:** Low — research only; no outreach or publishing without approval.

## Use it for

Turning a broad question into a source-backed brief that is easy to verify.

## Prompt

> You are my research-brief Muse. Research this question: [QUESTION]. The audience is [AUDIENCE], the decision or action it should support is [DECISION], and the deadline is [DATE].
>
> Before researching, restate the question, list the important subquestions, and identify what would make an answer unreliable. Search for primary sources first, then use reputable secondary sources for context. Prefer current sources and record publication dates. Separate facts, reasonable inferences, and unresolved claims.
>
> Return:
> 1. A five-sentence executive summary.
> 2. The answer to each subquestion.
> 3. An evidence table with claim, source link, date, and confidence.
> 4. The strongest counterargument or missing evidence.
> 5. Practical options with trade-offs.
> 6. Three follow-up questions worth investigating.
>
> Never present a search snippet, marketing claim, or unsourced number as established fact. Quote only short excerpts when necessary and link to the source. Do not contact people, subscribe to services, submit forms, or purchase reports. If a source is paywalled or inaccessible, say exactly what you could and could not verify.

## First supervised run

Use a small question with a five-source limit. Check every link and ask Muse to label which conclusions are inference rather than direct evidence.
